Week 7: Order-to-Cash: Quotes, Sales Orders & Invoicing

This week is where the entity records from Week 5 and the items from Week 6 finally meet a customer's money. Order-to-cash is the chain of transactions a sales deal actually walks through in NetSuite — estimate, sales order, fulfillment, invoice, payment — and each step is its own record type with its own status, linked back to the one before it. Once you can read that chain fluently, almost every "where did this number come from" question in sales operations becomes a matter of following the trail one transaction at a time.

By the end of this week, you'll be able to

  • Trace a deal through estimate → sales order → fulfillment → invoice and explain what changes at each step
  • Fulfill a sales order fully or partially and explain what a partial fulfillment leaves open
  • Read a customer's "money owed" picture from their invoices, payments and balance

1. Estimate → Sales Order → Fulfillment → Invoice

Order-to-cash in NetSuite isn't one record that changes status — it's a chain of separate transaction records, each one created ("transformed") from the one before it, each keeping its own line items, status and audit trail. An estimate (quote) is non-binding and doesn't touch inventory or the general ledger at all; it's purely a proposal. Converting it into a sales order is the real commitment — inventory gets committed (reserved) against it, but nothing is shipped or billed yet.

From there, a fulfillment (Item Fulfillment record) represents the physical act of shipping, and an invoice represents the financial act of billing. Those two can happen in either order, or together — a retail business might fulfill and invoice in the same motion, while a services or build-to-order business might invoice a deposit before anything ships.

the order-to-cash chain
ESTIMATE  ---(convert)-->  SALES ORDER  ---(fulfill)-->  ITEM FULFILLMENT
(quote,                    (committed,                   (shipped,
not binding)                not shipped/billed)           inventory decremented)
                                 |
                                 +----(bill)-->  INVOICE  ---(receive payment)-->  PAYMENT
                                                  (A/R created,                     (A/R cleared)
                                                   GL posts)

Every downstream transaction keeps a link back to the one it came from — open any invoice and you can click through to the sales order, and from there back to the original estimate. That chain is what makes a sales order's status meaningful: "Pending Fulfillment," "Partially Fulfilled," "Pending Billing," "Billed" — each status is really just a summary of how far along the chain that order has gotten.

"Transform" is the verb, not "convert" on every screen

NetSuite's UI calls it "Transform" when you turn an estimate into a sales order, or a sales order into an invoice. Looking for a "Transform" button (usually at the top of the record) is the fastest way to move a deal to its next stage without re-typing every line item by hand.

2. Pick, Pack, Ship & Partial Fulfillments

Fulfilling a sales order is where inventory actually leaves the warehouse. On the Item Fulfillment record, each line shows the quantity ordered and lets you enter the quantity you're actually shipping right now — these don't have to match. Shipping 6 of 10 ordered units creates a partial fulfillment: those 6 units decrement inventory and the order's status becomes "Partially Fulfilled," while the remaining 4 stay committed, waiting for a second fulfillment later.

Larger warehouse operations break fulfillment itself into sub-steps — pick (gather the physical items), pack (box them), ship (hand off to a carrier) — sometimes as separate statuses on the same Item Fulfillment record, sometimes with pick tickets and packing slips printed along the way. The underlying data model stays the same regardless: one fulfillment record per shipment event, however many shipment events a single sales order ends up needing.

a sales order fulfilled in two shipments
SALES ORDER #2050:  10x Widget A

  FULFILLMENT #1 (Mon): ships 6x Widget A
    -> inventory -6, order status: Partially Fulfilled

  FULFILLMENT #2 (Thu): ships remaining 4x Widget A
    -> inventory -4, order status: Pending Billing (fully fulfilled)
Committed inventory isn't the same as on-hand inventory

A sales order commits inventory the moment it's saved, before a single unit ships. If a saved search or report only looks at "quantity on hand," it will overstate what's actually available to sell — the number that matters for taking new orders is quantity on hand minus quantity already committed.

3. Invoicing, Payments & the Customer "Money Owed" Picture

An invoice is what actually posts to the general ledger — it debits accounts receivable and credits revenue, and it's the document a customer pays against. Depending on how a business bills, an invoice might be created from a fully fulfilled sales order, from a partial fulfillment, or even ahead of fulfillment for a deposit. Whatever the trigger, the invoice is the transaction that starts the clock on when a customer owes money and by when.

A customer payment is applied against one or more open invoices, reducing the customer's outstanding balance. NetSuite can apply a payment automatically (oldest invoice first) or let a user pick exactly which invoices a payment covers — important when a customer pays a specific invoice amount rather than "whatever I owe." The customer record's balance field, and reports like A/R Aging, are both just rollups of open invoices minus applied payments.

reading a customer's balance
CUSTOMER: Acme Corp
  Invoice #9001   $1,200   (open)
  Invoice #9002     $800   (open)
  Payment  #P-55   -$800   (applied to Invoice #9002)
  --------------------------------
  Balance:        $1,200   (Invoice #9001 still open)
"Unapplied payment" is a real, searchable state

If a payment is entered but never matched to an invoice, it sits as an unapplied credit on the customer — it reduces their balance in total but doesn't close any specific invoice. Collections teams watch for these because they often mean a payment was recorded in a hurry and needs to be reconciled against the right invoice later.

4. Hands-on Exercise

Hands-on

Run one deal through the entire order-to-cash chain

Using the customer and item you created in Weeks 5 and 6 (or any existing records in your sandbox), walk one deal all the way from quote to cash.

Part 1 — Quote to commitment:

  1. Create an Estimate for your test customer with at least 2 line items, one with a quantity of 10 or more.
  2. Transform the Estimate into a Sales Order. Note what the order's status reads immediately after saving.

Part 2 — Partial fulfillment:

  1. Fulfill the sales order, but ship less than the full quantity on at least one line (e.g. 6 of 10).
  2. Confirm the order's status now reads "Partially Fulfilled," then create a second fulfillment for the remaining quantity and confirm the status changes again.

Part 3 — Bill and collect:

  1. Create an invoice from the fully fulfilled sales order.
  2. Record a customer payment for less than the full invoice amount, and check the customer's balance afterward.
  3. Open the invoice and use its link back to the originating sales order, then from there back to the original estimate — confirm you can navigate the whole chain in both directions.
No live account handy?

The same exercise works as a paper exercise: sketch the four transaction boxes (estimate, sales order, fulfillment, invoice) on paper and draw the links between them, labeling what quantity and dollar amount sits on each one at each step. You'll still build the mental model, just without clicking through screens.

5. Knowledge Check

5 quick questions. Expand each to check your answer.

Q1

Why doesn't creating an Estimate commit any inventory or post anything to the general ledger?

An Estimate is a non-binding quote — the customer hasn't committed to buy yet, so NetSuite doesn't reserve stock or create any financial transaction for it. Only transforming it into a Sales Order represents a real commitment worth reserving inventory against.

Q2

A sales order for 10 units is fulfilled with only 6 units shipped so far. What happens to the order's status, and what happens to the remaining 4 units?

The order status becomes "Partially Fulfilled." The remaining 4 units stay committed (reserved) against that order, waiting for a second Item Fulfillment to ship them later.

Q3

Which transaction in the order-to-cash chain is the one that actually posts to the general ledger (debits A/R, credits revenue)?

The invoice. Estimates, sales orders and item fulfillments all affect inventory commitments or physical shipment, but it's the invoice that creates the accounts-receivable and revenue entries in the general ledger.

Q4

A customer pays $800 against a $1,200 invoice. What does their balance show afterward, and is the invoice considered closed?

Their balance shows $400 still owed on that invoice, and the invoice remains open (not fully paid) until the remaining $400 is collected.

Q5

What does it mean for a customer payment to be "unapplied," and why would a collections team care?

It means the payment was recorded but never matched to a specific invoice — it lowers the customer's total balance but doesn't close any particular invoice. Collections teams watch for these because they usually signal a payment that still needs to be reconciled against the correct invoice.
