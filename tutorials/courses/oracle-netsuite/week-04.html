Week 4: Saved Searches & Reporting Basics

If Week 1's navigation skills are the most-used day-to-day habit in NetSuite, the saved search is the most-used tool — the single feature that turns "I wonder how many..." into a real, reusable answer without asking IT for a custom report. This week closes out Module 1 by teaching you to filter records down to exactly the ones that matter, turn a flat list into grouped totals, and know when a saved search is the right tool versus when you actually want a financial report instead.

Module 1 of 6 Week 4 of 26 ~3 Hours Hands-on Exercise Included

By the end of this week, you'll be able to

  • Build a saved search with the right criteria and results columns to answer a specific business question
  • Use summary types to turn a flat list of records into grouped totals, sums, averages or counts
  • Schedule or share a saved search appropriately, and know when to reach for a financial report instead

1. Criteria, Filters & Results Columns

Every saved search starts at Lists → Search → Saved Searches → New, where the first choice — the base record type (Sales Order, Customer, Item, Transaction) — decides what the search is even capable of returning. From there, two subtabs do two genuinely different jobs, and confusing them is the single most common saved-search mistake beginners make.

The Criteria subtab decides which records qualify at all — conditions like "Status is Pending Fulfillment" or "Date is within the last 30 days" are applied before anything is displayed, and a record that fails any criterion simply never appears in the results. The Results subtab decides what you see and how it's arranged for the records that already qualified — which fields become columns, their order, and the sort. Adding a field to Results does not narrow anything down; it only changes what's visible.

criteria narrows the set · results only displays it
Saved Search: "Open Sales Orders This Month"
Base record: Sales Order

CRITERIA (which records qualify)
  Status        is          Pending Fulfillment
  Date          is within   This Month

RESULTS (what you see, for the records that qualified)
  Columns:  Customer | Date | Amount | Status
  Sort by:  Date (descending)

→ Adding "Amount" to Results shows the amount.
→ Adding "Amount is greater than 1000" to Criteria removes
  every order of $1000 or less from the results entirely.

Advanced search mode also unlocks formula columns — Formula (Numeric), Formula (Text), Formula (Date) — which compute a value with a SQL-like expression instead of pulling a stored field directly, useful for things like "days since order date" that no standard field holds.

"Available Filters" lets viewers refine without editing your search

Fields added as Available Filters show up as dropdowns on the search results page itself, letting a viewer narrow results further on their own — without touching the Criteria you defined as the search's author. Use it when different viewers need different slices of the same base search.

2. Summary Types: Group, Sum, Average & Count

By default, a saved search returns one row per record — exactly as granular as the data itself. Checking "Use Summary Type" on the Results subtab turns that flat list into an aggregated report: instead of a hundred individual sales orders, you get one row per customer with a total.

Each results column gets its own summary type once this is turned on: Group collapses rows that share the same value into a single row (e.g. one row per Customer), while Sum, Average, Count, Minimum and Maximum compute an aggregate for every other column within each group. At least one column needs Group for the others to have something to aggregate into.

flat results vs. grouped-and-summarized results
WITHOUT summary types (one row per sales order)
Customer       Date         Amount
Acme Corp      2026-03-01   $500
Acme Corp      2026-03-04   $1,200
Beta LLC       2026-03-02   $900

WITH summary types (Customer: Group · Amount: Sum)
Customer       Amount (Sum)
Acme Corp      $1,700
Beta LLC       $900
"Every row is still separate" almost always means no column is set to Group

Turning on "Use Summary Type" does nothing by itself — if not even one results column has its summary type set to Group, the search just shows the same flat rows with an aggregate label attached to each one individually. Set exactly the column(s) you want to collapse by to Group first, then assign Sum/Average/Count to the rest.

3. Scheduling & Sharing a Saved Search; Saved Search vs. Report

The Available subtab on a saved search controls who else can see it and how it reaches them: marking it Public shares it with every user, or you can restrict access to specific roles; "Email" options let you schedule the results to be emailed on a recurring basis; and a search can be added as a portlet on a dashboard so its results sit on someone's homepage automatically, no manual re-running required.

It's worth being precise about sharing scope: a saved search can expose data its viewer wouldn't otherwise see through the UI (role permissions still apply to the underlying records, but a well-built search can still surface more than intended at a glance). Treat "who can run this search" with the same care you'll apply to actual role design in Week 10.

Finally, know when to reach for a financial report instead of a saved search. Financial reports (built with the Financial Report Builder, not the saved search tool) are purpose-built for formatted, GL-based statements — a Profit & Loss, a Balance Sheet, period-over-period comparison columns, subtotals that follow accounting rules. A saved search is the right tool for "find me records matching X"; a report is the right tool for "produce a proper accounting statement."

saved search vs. financial report, at a glance
SAVED SEARCH                          FINANCIAL REPORT
-------------                         ----------------
Any record type                      GL-based statements only
Ad hoc, author-defined criteria      Standardized accounting structure
Great for operational questions      Great for P&L, Balance Sheet,
("orders still pending")             period comparisons, audit-ready output
Built in minutes, easy to tweak      Built with the Report Builder,
                                      follows accounting period rules
"Does this need to tie to the general ledger?" is the deciding question

If the answer is yes — it needs to match accounting periods, subtotal the way a financial statement does, or be audit-ready — reach for a report. If the answer is no — it's an operational question about records and their fields — a saved search is almost always faster to build and easier to adjust later.

4. Hands-on Exercise

Hands-on

Build, group and share a saved search

Use your SuiteTraining account or sandbox.

Part 1 — Build a criteria-driven search:

  1. Create a saved search on Sales Order (or Transaction) with criteria matching an open/pending status.
  2. Add results columns for Customer, Date and Amount, sorted by Date descending.
  3. Save, run it, and confirm every row matches your criteria.

Part 2 — Group and summarize:

  1. Duplicate the search from Part 1 (or edit a copy).
  2. Turn on "Use Summary Type," set Customer to Group and Amount to Sum.
  3. Confirm the results now show one row per customer with a total, not one row per order.

Part 3 — Schedule and scope sharing:

  1. On the Available subtab, schedule the grouped search to email to yourself on a weekly recurrence.
  2. Set sharing to a specific role rather than Public, and explain in one sentence why that scoping matters.

5. Knowledge Check

5 quick questions. Expand each to check your answer.

Q1

You add "Amount" as a results column but the search still shows orders under $1000. What should you check?

Whether an Amount condition was actually added to the Criteria subtab. Adding a field to Results only displays it — it doesn't filter anything. To exclude orders under $1000, the condition needs to be on Criteria, not Results.

Q2

What does the "Group" summary type do, and why does at least one column usually need it?

Group collapses every row sharing the same value in that column into a single row. Other summary types like Sum or Average need a Group column to define what they're aggregating into — without one, there's nothing for the aggregates to collapse around.

Q3

What's the difference between a results column and an "Available Filter"?

A results column just displays a field's value. An Available Filter adds a dropdown on the search results page itself, letting a viewer further narrow the results on their own, without editing the search's own Criteria.

Q4

A stakeholder asks for a Profit & Loss statement with period-over-period comparison. Saved search or report, and why?

A financial report. It needs to tie to the general ledger and follow standardized accounting period structure and subtotals — exactly what the Financial Report Builder is designed for, and not what an ad hoc saved search is meant to produce.

Q5

Why does it matter whether you share a saved search as Public versus restricted to specific roles?

A saved search can surface data at a glance in ways that go beyond what a viewer's role permissions would normally expose through standard navigation, so sharing scope deserves the same care as role design — which Week 10 covers in depth.
