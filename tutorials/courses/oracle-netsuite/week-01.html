Week 1: Welcome to NetSuite: The SuiteCloud Platform

Before touching a single record, it helps to know what kind of system you're actually looking at. This week covers what NetSuite is and why companies run their whole business on it instead of a pile of separate tools, how a "role" quietly decides both what you're allowed to do and what the screen even shows you, and how to find your way around any record using lists, forms, sublists and the search bar — the three skills every later week assumes you already have.

Module 1 of 6 Week 1 of 26 ~2.5 Hours Hands-on Exercise Included

By the end of this week, you'll be able to

  • Explain what NetSuite is and why a company would consolidate onto it instead of using separate tools
  • Describe how a role determines both permissions and the center (workspace) a user sees
  • Navigate any record confidently using lists, forms, sublists, subtabs and global search

1. What Is NetSuite?

NetSuite is a cloud-based, multi-tenant business management system — ERP (enterprise resource planning), CRM (customer relationship management) and e-commerce, running on one shared platform and one database instead of several disconnected tools. "Multi-tenant" means every customer's company runs on the same underlying application code, in the cloud, with their own data kept separate — nobody installs a server, and everybody is always on the current version.

Most growing companies start with separate tools for separate jobs: accounting software for the books, a CRM for the sales pipeline, a spreadsheet for inventory, a shopping-cart platform for the web store. That works for a while, and then the gaps between those tools start costing real time and real mistakes:

separate tools vs. one platform
SEPARATE TOOLS                         ONE PLATFORM (NetSuite)
accounting software  ---\
CRM                   ---+-- nightly    a single sales order updates
spreadsheet inventory ---+   exports/   inventory, triggers the invoice,
e-commerce platform   ---/   imports,   and posts to the ledger — in the
                              and gaps  SAME transaction, instantly

This is why NetSuite's own name for its platform is SuiteCloud: a single data model and a single set of records that every module — sales, support, inventory, accounting, HR — reads from and writes to. A sales order doesn't need to be "synced" to the accounting system; the accounting system is the same database the sales order was just saved into. That's the one idea worth carrying into every later week: almost every feature you'll meet is a different view onto the same underlying records, not a separate system bolted on.

"NetSuite" is one product, used very differently by different teams

An accountant, a warehouse clerk and a sales rep can all be looking at the same NetSuite account and barely recognize each other's screens. That's by design, and it's exactly what Section 2 explains.

2. Roles, Centers & Dashboards

Every user logs into NetSuite with one or more roles — "Accounts Payable Clerk," "Sales Rep," "Administrator," and so on. A role does two jobs at once, and it's easy to only notice the first one:

  • Permissions: which records a role can view, create, edit, or has full access to — the security half, covered properly in Week 10.
  • Center: which workspace a role sees after login — its own tailored homepage, menu structure and default dashboard, built around that job's day-to-day tasks.
same account, different centers
ROLE: Sales Rep            ROLE: Accounts Payable       ROLE: Administrator
----------------           ----------------------       -------------------
Sales Center               Accounting Center            Classic/Admin Center
- My pipeline              - Bills to pay                - Full menu tree
- Quotas & quick quote    - Vendor balances             - Setup & customization
- Customer list            - Payment runs                - Every record type

A dashboard is the homepage inside a center, built from portlets — small panels like "Reminders," "Recent Records," a KPI scorecard, or a saved search's results. Every user can rearrange, add or remove portlets on their own dashboard without touching anyone else's, and an administrator can also publish a standard dashboard layout that a whole role starts from.

A single person often holds more than one role — a controller might have both "Accountant" and "Administrator." NetSuite shows a role switcher after login (or in the account menu) so they can move between the two without logging out, and each switch changes both what they're allowed to do and what the screen looks like.

"I can't find that menu item" is almost always a role problem

If a feature or record type is missing from the menu, it's rarely broken — the active role usually just doesn't have permission to see it, or its center simply doesn't surface that menu path. Switching to a broader role (like Administrator) to confirm a feature exists at all is a fast way to tell "I don't have access" apart from "this isn't set up."

3. Navigating Records: Lists, Forms, Sublists & Global Search

Everything NetSuite stores — a customer, an invoice, an item, an employee — is a record. Week 2 goes deep on what a record is actually made of; this week is just about finding and opening one.

A list is a filterable, sortable table of records of one type — "Customers," "Sales Orders," "Items." Clicking a row opens that record's form: the detailed view with fields for that single record, arranged on labeled tabs. Most forms also contain one or more sublists — a small table living inside the form, such as the list of line items on a sales order — and those sublists are themselves often organized across subtabs.

anatomy of a sales order form
LIST: "Sales Orders"  →  click a row  →  FORM opens

  FORM: Sales Order #1042
  +-----------------------------------------------+
  | Customer: Acme Corp      Date: 2026-03-01      |  <- body fields
  | Status: Pending Fulfillment                     |
  +-----------------------------------------------+
  | [Items] [Shipping] [Billing] [Communication]    |  <- subtabs
  |  Item          Qty   Rate    Amount             |  <- SUBLIST
  |  Widget A       10   $5.00   $50.00             |     (lives inside
  |  Widget B        4   $12.50  $50.00             |      the Items subtab)
  +-----------------------------------------------+

Forms open in view mode by default (read-only, faster to load) with an Edit button to switch into an editable form. Every page also has a global search bar at the top — typing a customer name, an invoice number, or even part of an item name jumps straight to matching records, which is almost always faster than clicking through a list and its filters.

The breadcrumb trail at the top of a form is a shortcut, not decoration

It shows the list you came from and lets you jump back to it with your filters still applied, instead of re-navigating the menu from scratch every time you open a record.

4. Hands-on Exercise

Hands-on

Explore a live NetSuite account as two different roles

This week's exercise runs inside an actual NetSuite account — a free SuiteTraining account or a company sandbox both work. Unlike most later weeks, there's no code yet, just deliberate exploration.

Part 1 — Compare two centers:

  1. Log in and note which center and dashboard you land on by default.
  2. If you have access to more than one role, switch roles and write down three concrete differences between the two dashboards (portlets present, menu items available, default landing page).
  3. If you only have one role, open Setup → Users/Roles → Manage Roles (or ask whoever administers the account) and read through what a different role, like "Sales Rep," is configured to show.

Part 2 — Navigate a record end to end:

  1. Open any list of entity records (e.g. Customers) and open one record into its form.
  2. Identify at least 3 body fields, 2 subtabs, and 1 sublist on that form.
  3. Switch the record into Edit mode, then back to view mode without saving, and confirm you can tell the two modes apart at a glance.

Part 3 — Use global search:

  1. Use the global search bar to find the same record from Part 2 by typing only part of its name.
  2. Use the breadcrumb at the top of the form to return to the list you started from, and confirm your previous filters are still applied.

5. Knowledge Check

5 quick questions. Expand each to check your answer.

Q1

What does "multi-tenant" mean in the context of NetSuite, and what does it free a company from needing to do?

Every customer company runs on the same shared underlying application code in the cloud, with each company's data kept separate. It frees a company from installing or maintaining its own servers, and everyone is automatically kept on the current version.

Q2

Why doesn't a sales order need to be "synced" to the accounting side of NetSuite the way it would between two separate tools?

Because sales and accounting read from and write to the same underlying database and data model — there's no separate accounting system to copy the sales order into, so there's nothing to sync.

Q3

Name the two separate jobs a role does, beyond just "what this user is allowed to click."

Permissions (what records a role can view, create, edit or has full access to) and the center (which tailored workspace, menu structure and default dashboard the user sees after login).

Q4

On a sales order form, is the table of line items (item, quantity, rate) a body field, a sublist, or a subtab?

A sublist — a small table of rows living inside the form, as opposed to a single body field like "Customer" or "Date," and typically organized under a subtab such as "Items."

Q5

A menu item you expect to see is missing. What's the fastest first thing to check, and why?

Check whether the active role has permission to see it (or switch to a broader role like Administrator to confirm the feature exists at all). A missing menu item is almost always a role/permission limitation, not a sign the feature is unavailable or broken.
