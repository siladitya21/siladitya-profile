Week 24: OneWorld: Multi-Subsidiary & Consolidated Operations

Everything so far has implicitly assumed one company, one set of books, one currency. OneWorld is the edition of NetSuite that drops that assumption, running many legal entities — subsidiaries — inside a single account with its own books, currency and compliance requirements each, while still rolling up to one consolidated view for the parent. This week closes out the integration and reporting module by covering the piece that touches almost everything built before it: subsidiaries, intercompany transactions and multi-currency consolidation.

Module 5 of 6 Week 24 of 26 ~3 Hours Hands-on Exercise Included

By the end of this week, you'll be able to

  • Explain how subsidiaries relate to each other and how intercompany transactions are eliminated on consolidation
  • Describe how multi-currency consolidation works and why local compliance still varies by subsidiary
  • Design roles and permissions that respect subsidiary boundaries in a OneWorld account

1. Subsidiaries, Intercompany Transactions & Elimination

A subsidiary in OneWorld is a distinct legal entity — its own chart of accounts (inheriting from a shared parent structure), its own base currency, its own tax nexus — nested in a hierarchy under a parent company. Nearly every record carries a subsidiary field, and most roles are scoped to one or a handful of subsidiaries, so a user at the UK subsidiary generally can't see US-only transactions by default.

a simple subsidiary hierarchy
Acme Global (parent, consolidated reporting)
├─ Acme US        (base currency: USD)
├─ Acme UK Ltd    (base currency: GBP)
└─ Acme GmbH      (base currency: EUR)

An intercompany transaction happens when one subsidiary sells to, or bills, another — Acme US manufacturing and selling inventory to Acme UK for resale, for example. Left alone, that transaction would inflate consolidated revenue (the group selling to itself looks like extra revenue that never left the company). Elimination is the consolidation step that reverses exactly those intercompany balances out of the group-level financials, so the parent's consolidated P&L reflects only revenue earned from genuinely external customers.

Intercompany transactions still need to be entered correctly on both sides

NetSuite can automate elimination, but only if the intercompany sale is actually recorded as such on both subsidiaries' books. A transaction that should be intercompany but gets entered as a regular sale breaks the elimination and overstates consolidated revenue.

2. Multi-Currency Consolidation & Local Compliance

Each subsidiary transacts and keeps its books in its own base currency, but the parent needs one consolidated set of financials. NetSuite handles this by translating each subsidiary's balances into the parent's reporting currency using exchange rates — typically the rate as of the period close for balance sheet items, and an average rate for the period for income statement items — and rolling the translated results up the hierarchy.

translation vs. transaction currency
Acme UK Ltd books a sale       → recorded in GBP (its base currency)
Consolidated report at parent  → GBP translated to USD using period
                                   exchange rates, then rolled up with
                                   Acme US (already USD) and Acme GmbH
                                   (EUR, also translated)

Consolidation doesn't erase local requirements, though: each subsidiary still needs its own statutory reports, its own tax filings, and often its own fiscal calendar — OneWorld supports per-subsidiary tax nexuses and localized formats precisely because "one global company" on paper is still several legally distinct entities each answering to a different regulator.

Don't assume one customization fits every subsidiary

A workflow or form built around US tax rules can silently produce wrong results for a subsidiary with a different tax regime. Customizations in a OneWorld account should be tested against every subsidiary they're deployed to, not just the one they were built for.

3. Role & Permission Design Across Subsidiaries

Week 10's role design gets a new dimension in OneWorld: a role now also has a subsidiary restriction — "this subsidiary only," "this subsidiary and its children," or "all subsidiaries" — layered on top of its usual record-level permissions. A controller who needs to see consolidated numbers across every subsidiary needs a broader restriction than an AP clerk who should only ever touch one entity's bills.

subsidiary restriction examples
Role: AP Clerk (Acme UK)        → restricted to Acme UK Ltd only
Role: Regional Controller       → Acme UK Ltd + its children
Role: Group CFO / Administrator → all subsidiaries (consolidated view)

Getting this wrong in either direction causes real problems: too narrow, and people doing legitimate cross-subsidiary work (a shared-services team invoicing on behalf of multiple entities) keep hitting access walls; too broad, and someone in one country's AP team can see another country's payroll-adjacent vendor bills they have no business reason to touch. The right design starts from who legitimately needs to see across a boundary, not from "just give everyone Administrator to avoid tickets."

Subsidiary restriction and record-level permission are independent dials

A role can have full permission on vendor bills but still be restricted to one subsidiary — the two settings compose, so testing a role in just one subsidiary can hide a permission problem that only shows up once the role is actually used in another.

4. Hands-on Exercise

Hands-on

Trace a subsidiary structure and design a role

A OneWorld sandbox or demo account with at least two subsidiaries is ideal; if you don't have one, this exercise works as a paper design exercise using the hierarchy in this week's first code block.

Part 1 — Explore the structure:

  1. Go to Setup → Company → Subsidiaries (or review the sample hierarchy above) and note each subsidiary's base currency and parent relationship.
  2. Find one example of an intercompany transaction type (e.g. Intercompany Sales Order) and identify both sides it posts to.

Part 2 — Consolidation:

  1. Open a consolidated financial report (or review a sample one) and identify where elimination of intercompany balances would apply.
  2. Explain, in your own words, what would happen to consolidated revenue if an intercompany sale were accidentally entered as a regular external sale.

Part 3 — Design a role:

  1. Design a role for a "Regional AP Clerk" covering one subsidiary and its children: list its subsidiary restriction and the minimum record-level permissions it needs.
  2. Design a second role for a "Group Controller" who needs consolidated visibility, and write one sentence on how its subsidiary restriction differs from the AP Clerk's.

5. Knowledge Check

5 quick questions. Expand each to check your answer.

Q1

Why would consolidated revenue be overstated if an intercompany sale weren't eliminated?

Because the group selling to itself (one subsidiary to another) isn't real revenue from an external customer — counting it would inflate the consolidated P&L with money that never actually left the company.

Q2

What has to happen for NetSuite's automated elimination to work correctly on an intercompany transaction?

The transaction has to actually be entered as an intercompany transaction on both subsidiaries' books. If it's entered as a regular external sale instead, elimination has nothing to catch and consolidated revenue ends up overstated.

Q3

Which exchange rate convention does NetSuite typically use for balance sheet items vs. income statement items during consolidation?

Balance sheet items typically use the exchange rate as of the period close; income statement items typically use the average rate for the period, before both are rolled up into the parent's reporting currency.

Q4

Why does consolidation at the parent level not eliminate each subsidiary's need for its own statutory reports and tax filings?

Each subsidiary is still a legally distinct entity answering to its own regulator, with its own tax nexus and often its own fiscal calendar. "One global company" financially is still several separate legal entities with separate compliance obligations.

Q5

A role has full record-level permission on vendor bills but is restricted to one subsidiary. What does that combination mean in practice?

The role can fully view/create/edit vendor bills, but only within its one assigned subsidiary — subsidiary restriction and record-level permission are independent settings that compose together, not a single combined setting.
