Chapter 2: Cyber Safety

Based on the official CBSE Computer Applications (165) curriculum, Unit 2 — 15 marks. With networking and software covered, this chapter addresses the risks that come with actually using the Internet day to day: browsing safely, protecting your identity and accounts, respecting others' privacy, recognising cyberstalking, knowing how to report cybercrime, and the two malware types this syllabus names specifically.

Chapter 2 of 6 Unit 2 — 15 Marks ~2 Hours Exam-style Questions Included

By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to

  • List practical steps for safe browsing and social network use
  • Create a strong password and explain why privacy/confidentiality matter online
  • Recognise cyberstalking, know how to report cybercrime, and tell a virus from adware

2.1 Safe Web Browsing and Social Network Use

Habits that keep browsing safe
- Check for "https://" and a padlock icon before entering any
  personal or payment information on a website
- Don't click links or download attachments from unknown senders
- Keep your browser and antivirus software updated
- Download software/apps only from official, trusted sources
- Think before you post -- once something is online, it can be
  copied, screenshotted, or shared beyond your control
Safe social network habits
- Keep your profile/posts private, or visible only to people you
  actually know
- Don't accept friend/follow requests from strangers
- Never share your home address, school name, or daily schedule
  publicly
- Be cautious about tagging your exact location in posts/photos

2.2 Protecting Your Identity and Using Passwords Properly

What makes a password strong
- At least 8-12 characters long
- A MIX of uppercase, lowercase, numbers AND special characters
- NOT a common word, your name, or an easily guessed date (like a
  birthday)
- DIFFERENT for every important account -- so one leaked password
  doesn't unlock everything else
- Changed periodically, and immediately if you suspect it's been
  seen by someone else
- NEVER shared with anyone, even a friend
Weak vs. strong password — exam example

Weak: password123 or rahul2010 (predictable, personal, no variety). Strong: something like Tr#9kLm!2qW — unrelated to personal details, mixing character types, hard to guess or crack.

2.3 Privacy and Confidentiality of Information

Two related but distinct ideas
PRIVACY        your right to control who sees YOUR personal
               information (photos, location, messages, habits)

CONFIDENTIALITY  the obligation to keep information someone SHARED
                 WITH YOU protected, and not disclose it without
                 their permission (a friend's private message, a
                 classmate's phone number)

Respecting both means thinking twice before posting photos that include other people, forwarding someone's private message, or sharing information a friend trusted you with.

2.4 Cyberstalking and Reporting Cybercrime

Cyberstalking
Using the Internet (messages, social media, tracking apps, repeated
unwanted contact) to harass, intimidate, or monitor a person
persistently -- causing them fear or distress.

Warning signs: repeated unwanted messages, someone tracking your
online activity/location, fake accounts created to watch or
contact you after being blocked.
What to do — reporting cybercrime
- Do NOT respond to or engage with the harasser
- SAVE evidence -- screenshots, messages, usernames, dates/times
- BLOCK the person on the platform where it's happening
- Tell a trusted adult (parent/teacher) immediately
- Report it through the platform's own reporting tool
- For serious cases, India has a dedicated National Cyber Crime
  Reporting Portal where incidents can be formally reported

2.5 Malware — Viruses and Adware

The two malware types named in this syllabus
VIRUS
  Malicious code that attaches itself to a legitimate file/program
  and SPREADS by copying itself to other files/computers when that
  infected file is opened or shared. Can delete/corrupt data, slow
  the system down, or cause programs to malfunction.

ADWARE
  Software that automatically displays or downloads unwanted
  advertisements (pop-ups, banners) on your device. Usually less
  destructive than a virus, but can slow performance, track
  browsing habits without consent, or create an opening for other
  malware via unsafe ad links.
Basic protection
- Install and regularly update antivirus software
- Don't install pirated or unknown free software
- Avoid clicking pop-up ads, especially ones promising free prizes
- Scan any USB/pen drive before opening its files

6. Exercise (exam-style, based on the CBSE curriculum)

Unit 2 Practice Questions

Work through these on paper

  1. List any four safe browsing practices you should follow.
  2. What makes a password strong? Write one weak and one strong example password, and explain why.
  3. Differentiate between privacy and confidentiality with an example of each.
  4. What is cyberstalking? List three warning signs that someone might be a victim of it.
  5. You suspect a classmate is being cyberstalked online. List the steps you'd advise them to take.
  6. Differentiate between a computer virus and adware.
  7. Why should you avoid sharing your home address or daily schedule on a public social media profile?
  8. What information should you NEVER share with anyone, even a friend, regarding your online accounts?
  9. Name the official Indian portal where a cybercrime incident can be formally reported.
  10. List two precautions to take before inserting an unknown pen drive into your computer.
