Chapter 4: Office Tools — Presentation Software

The second of the three office tools in Unit 3 — presentation software (Microsoft PowerPoint or LibreOffice Impress). The exam cares less about flashy animations than about whether you understand slide structure, consistent formatting and how to deliver a presentation that actually lands.

Chapter 4 of 6 Unit 3, Part B ~2 Hours Exam-style Questions Included

By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to

  • Choose slide layouts, views and backgrounds for a presentation
  • Add headers/footers, slide numbers and pictures consistently
  • Apply animations and sound, and rehearse slide timings
1 Slide Layouts, Views & Backgrounds 2 Headers, Footers, Numbers & Pictures 3 Animations, Sound & Timings 4 Exercise

4.1 Slide Layouts, Views and Backgrounds

Choosing how a slide is arranged
SLIDE LAYOUT   a ready-made arrangement of placeholders --
               e.g. "Title Slide", "Title and Content",
               "Two Content", "Blank"

NORMAL VIEW    the main editing view -- one slide at a time,
               with a thumbnail panel on the side

SLIDE SORTER   shows ALL slides as small thumbnails in a grid --
  VIEW         useful for reordering or deleting slides quickly

HANDOUT VIEW   lays out multiple slides per printed page, for
               giving the audience something to follow along with

BACKGROUND     a solid colour, gradient, or image applied behind
               every slide's content for visual consistency

4.2 Headers, Footers, Slide Numbers and Pictures

Keeping slides consistent
HEADER/FOOTER   Insert -> Header & Footer; text like a date or
                presenter name repeated on every slide

SLIDE NUMBER    automatically numbers each slide, updating as
                slides are added, removed or reordered

INSERTING       Insert -> Picture; resize with a corner handle
PICTURES        (hold Shift to keep the aspect ratio) -- avoid
                stretching images out of proportion
Keep it readable

Use a consistent font and background across all slides, and avoid cramming more than a few bullet points onto one slide — the goal is to support what the presenter says, not replace it.

4.3 Animations, Sound Effects and Rehearsing Timings

Making a presentation move
ANIMATION       a visual effect applied to an object (e.g. text
                flying in, fading in) when it appears on screen --
                Animations tab -> choose an effect

SLIDE           the visual/sound effect used when moving FROM one
TRANSITION      slide TO the next -- Transitions tab

SOUND EFFECTS   audio played alongside an animation or transition,
                or a sound clip inserted into a slide

REHEARSE        Slide Show -> Rehearse Timings; practises your
TIMINGS         delivery and records how long each slide is shown,
                so the whole show can later auto-advance correctly

4. Exercise (exam-style, based on the CBSE curriculum)

Unit 3 (Part B) Practice Questions

Work through these on paper

  1. What is a slide layout? Name two common layouts.
  2. Differentiate between Normal view and Slide Sorter view.
  3. Why might a presenter use Handout view?
  4. What is the purpose of inserting a slide number into a presentation?
  5. Differentiate between an animation and a slide transition.
  6. What does "Rehearse Timings" do, and why is it useful?
  7. List two things you should avoid when designing a presentation's slides.
  8. How would you keep your resized picture from looking stretched or distorted?
  9. What is a background in presentation software, and why would you apply one consistently?
  10. Name one place sound effects can be attached to in a presentation.
