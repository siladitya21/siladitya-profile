Week 5: Present Tense Verbs & Daily Routine

Week 4 gave you sentences without action — this week adds real verbs. You'll learn the present tense conjugation pattern that every regular Kannada verb follows, a set of common daily-life verbs, and how to describe your own routine from morning to night.

Week 5 of 12 ~2.5 Hours Verb Conjugation Beginner-Friendly

By the end of this week, you'll be able to

  • Conjugate a regular verb in the present tense for any subject
  • Use a core set of common daily-life verbs
  • Describe a simple daily routine, start to finish

1. Present Tense Conjugation Patterns

Kannada verbs conjugate by attaching an ending to a verb stem, and the ending changes based on who's doing the action — much like Spanish or French, but with endings that stay genuinely regular across almost every verb. Take the stem ಮಾಡು (māḍu, "do"):

present tense of ಮಾಡು (māḍu — "to do")
ನಾನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ    Nānu māḍuttēne     I do
ನೀನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತೀಯ     Nīnu māḍuttīya     You do (informal)
ನೀವು ಮಾಡುತ್ತೀರಿ     Nīvu māḍuttīri     You do (formal)
ಅವನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾನೆ    Avanu māḍuttāne    He does
ಅವಳು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾಳೆ    Avaḷu māḍuttāḷe    She does
ನಾವು ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇವೆ    Nāvu māḍuttēve     We do
ಅವರು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾರೆ    Avaru māḍuttāre    They do

Every regular verb follows this exact same set of endings on its stem — swap ಮಾಡು for any other stem and the endings (-ತ್ತೇನೆ, -ತ್ತೀಯ, -ತ್ತೀರಿ, -ತ್ತಾನೆ, -ತ್ತಾಳೆ, -ತ್ತೇವೆ, -ತ್ತಾರೆ) stay the same. Learn this one table well and you can conjugate dozens of verbs immediately.

Spot the shared "-ತ್ತ-" core

Every single ending contains "-ttV-" (a "t" sound plus a vowel) before the person-specific tail — that shared piece is the present-tense marker itself. Recognising it will help you identify present-tense verbs on sight, even ones you haven't memorized yet.

2. Common Daily-Life Verbs

Here are the verb stems that will carry most of your daily-routine sentences. Attach the same endings from above to any of them.

common verb stems
ಮಾಡು    māḍu    do            ಕುಡಿ    kuḍi    drink
ತಿನ್ನು   tinnu   eat           ಬರೆ     bare    write
ಓದು     ōdu     read/study    ಬರು     baru    come
ಮಾತಾಡು  mātāḍu  speak/talk    ಹೋಗು    hōgu    go
ನೋಡು    nōḍu    see/watch     ನಿದ್ದೆ ಮಾಡು  nidde māḍu  sleep

Try conjugating "ಓದು" (ōdu, read) for yourself before continuing: "ನಾನು ಓದುತ್ತೇನೆ" (Nānu ōduttēne — "I read/study"). If that came out matching the pattern from Section 1, you've internalised the conjugation, not just memorized one example of it.

3. Describing Your Daily Routine

With verbs and time words (from Week 3) together, you can now narrate an entire day.

a simple daily routine
ನಾನು ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಎದ್ದೇನೆ           Nānu beḻigge eddēne         I wake up in the morning
ನಾನು ತಿಂಡಿ ತಿನ್ನುತ್ತೇನೆ         Nānu tiṇḍi tinnuttēne       I eat breakfast
ನಾನು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದುತ್ತೇನೆ          Nānu pustaka ōduttēne       I read a book
ನಾನು ಸಂಜೆ ಮಾತಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ         Nānu sanje mātāḍuttēne      I talk (with someone) in the evening
ನಾನು ರಾತ್ರಿ ನಿದ್ದೆ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ  Nānu rātri nidde māḍuttēne  I sleep at night

"ಎದ್ದೇನೆ" (eddēne, "I wake up") is a small irregular you'll simply memorize as a fixed form — a handful of very common verbs like this don't follow the regular pattern exactly, the same way English has "go/went" instead of "goed." Everything else in this table is fully regular.

4. Hands-on Exercise

Hands-on

Narrate your actual day

Build a five-sentence description of a real, ordinary day of yours using this week's tools.

  1. Write one sentence for morning, using ಮಾಡು, ತಿನ್ನು or ಎದ್ದೇನೆ.
  2. Write one sentence for midday, using ಓದು, ಬರೆ or ಮಾತಾಡು.
  3. Write one sentence for evening, using ನೋಡು, ಕುಡಿ or ಬರು/ಹೋಗು.
  4. Write one sentence for night, using ನಿದ್ದೆ ಮಾಡು.
  5. Conjugate every verb yourself from the stem — don't copy a conjugated form directly from the charts above.
Conjugate from the stem, not from memory of a whole word

If you only ever copy "māḍuttēne" as a fixed chunk, you won't be able to produce "ōduttēne" or "kuḍiyuttēne" later — building each conjugated form from its stem is what makes the pattern generalise.

5. Practice Quiz

Five questions covering conjugation and daily-routine vocabulary. Attempt each before expanding the answer.

Q1

What is the present-tense ending for "ಅವಳು" (she) on a regular verb stem like ಮಾಡು?

A) -ತ್ತೇನೆ (-ttēne)
B) -ತ್ತಾನೆ (-ttāne)
C) -ತ್ತಾಳೆ (-ttāḷe)
D) -ತ್ತೀರಿ (-ttīri)

Correct answer: C) -ತ್ತಾಳೆ (-ttāḷe). -ttāne is for "he" (avanu); -ttāḷe is the "she" (avaḷu) ending.

Q2

"Nānu pustaka ōduttēne" means:

A) I write a letter
B) I read a book
C) I watch a movie
D) I drink water

Correct answer: B) I read a book. "Pustaka" is book, and "ōduttēne" is the "I" form of ಓದು (read/study).

Q3

Which sound sequence appears in every present-tense ending, marking the tense itself?

A) -inda-
B) -tt-
C) -vāra-
D) -ge-

Correct answer: B) -tt-. Every present-tense ending contains this "t" sound plus a vowel before the person-specific tail.

Q4

Which verb stem means "to sleep"?

A) ನೋಡು (nōḍu)
B) ನಿದ್ದೆ ಮಾಡು (nidde māḍu)
C) ಬರು (baru)
D) ಮಾತಾಡು (mātāḍu)

Correct answer: B) ನಿದ್ದೆ ಮಾಡು (nidde māḍu). Literally "do sleep" — a two-word verb phrase for "sleep."

Q5

Why is "ಎದ್ದೇನೆ" (eddēne, "I wake up") worth memorizing as a fixed form?

A) It's not actually a verb
B) It's only used formally
C) It's an irregular verb that doesn't follow the regular conjugation pattern exactly
D) It only applies to women

Correct answer: C) It's an irregular verb. A handful of very common verbs, like "wake up," don't conjugate exactly like the regular pattern — much like English "go/went."
