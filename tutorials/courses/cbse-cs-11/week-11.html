Chapter 11: Societal Impact

Based on NCERT's Class XI Computer Science textbook — the final chapter. Digital technologies bring real benefits, but also real risks: this chapter covers digital footprints, what it means to be a responsible netizen, Intellectual Property Rights (copyright, patent, trademark) and their violation (plagiarism, infringement), free and open-source software, the common categories of cyber crime, India's IT Act, and the health impact of heavy digital-device use.

Chapter 11 of 11 — Final Chapter NCERT pp. 229–250 ~3 Hours Exercise Included

By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to

  • Distinguish active vs. passive digital footprints, and name the three categories of netizen etiquette
  • Tell copyright, patent and trademark apart, and recognise plagiarism vs. copyright infringement in a scenario
  • Name common cyber crimes (hacking, phishing, identity theft, ransomware) and basic prevention steps

11.1–11.2 Introduction & Digital Footprints

Every online activity — searching, buying a ticket, replying to an email, checking a score — leaves a trail of data: your digital footprint. It includes websites visited, emails sent, information submitted, plus your device's IP address, location and other specifics. It can be created and used with or without your knowledge.

Active vs. Passive footprints
ACTIVE   data you INTENTIONALLY submit -- emails you write, posts/
         comments you make on a website or app

PASSIVE  data generated WITHOUT your direct intention -- created
         just by visiting a website, using an app, browsing
No permanent deletion

Most footprint data sits on the remote server hosting the app, not just your browser — you generally cannot access or control it. Even deleting your own post doesn't erase it from the digital footprint; this is exactly how many cyber criminals are later traced.

11.3 Digital Society and Netizen

Anyone using digital technology with the Internet is a netizen. A responsible netizen follows three categories of etiquette:

11.3.1 — Net Etiquettes: Be Ethical, Respectful, Responsible
BE ETHICAL
  - No copyright violation (don't use copyrighted material without permission)
  - Share expertise -- but only when sure of its accuracy

BE RESPECTFUL
  - Respect privacy -- don't share others' private images/files without consent
  - Respect diversity -- of knowledge, experience, culture in a group/forum

BE RESPONSIBLE
  - Avoid cyber bullying -- repeated online behaviour meant to hurt/embarrass
    (rumours, threats, publishing a victim's personal info, harassment)
  - Don't feed the troll -- trolls thrive on attention, so ignore them
11.3.2 — Communication Etiquettes: Be Precise, Polite, Credible
BE PRECISE   respect others' TIME (don't expect instant replies) and
              DATA LIMITS (avoid huge attachments -- use cloud links instead)
BE POLITE    stay non-aggressive whether communication is synchronous
              (chat, calls) or asynchronous (email, forum posts)
BE CREDIBLE  your past comments/posts build (or damage) your credibility
              over time -- others judge your reliability by them
11.3.3 — Social Media Etiquettes: Be Secure, Be Reliable
BE SECURE
  - Choose a strong password, change it often, never share credentials
  - Know who you befriend -- unknown "friends" may have malicious intent
  - Beware of fake information -- validate news/posts before believing them

BE RELIABLE
  - Think before uploading -- content stays on the remote server even
    after you "delete" it locally

11.4 Data Protection & Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

Sensitive data — biometric, health, financial, personal documents — can cause real harm if breached, so it's protected via encryption, authentication and legal data-protection policies. Intellectual Property is an original idea, invention, or creative/artistic work — legally protected three ways:

Copyright, Patent, Trademark — side by side
COPYRIGHT
  - Covers: writing, photos, audio, video, software, art
  - Granted AUTOMATICALLY to the creator -- no filing needed
  - Prevents others copying/using/selling the work without a license

PATENT
  - Covers: INVENTIONS
  - Must be APPLIED FOR (filed) -- not automatic
  - Gives full control over who can use/sell/distribute it
  - Lasts 20 years, then becomes free to use

TRADEMARK
  - Covers: visual symbol, word, name, design, slogan, label (a BRAND)
  - Stops confusingly similar marks too (e.g. "Nikke" vs "Nike")
  - Can even be registered for UNRELATED goods by the same brand owner
11.4.2 — Three ways IPR is violated
PLAGIARISM
  Presenting someone else's idea/work as your own.
  Still plagiarism even if content is "free for public use" --
  you must still CITE the source/author.

COPYRIGHT INFRINGEMENT
  Using someone's work WITHOUT permission (or without paying, if sold) --
  even citing the source does NOT excuse this if usage wasn't permitted.
  Being "on the Internet" never automatically means "free to use."

TRADEMARK INFRINGEMENT
  Unauthorised use of someone's registered trademark on products/services.
Plagiarism vs. Copyright Infringement — the key exam distinction

Plagiarism is an ethical failure: not giving credit, even for content you're allowed to use. Copyright infringement is a legal one: using content without the owner's permission or payment — citing the source doesn't fix this if usage itself wasn't authorised.

11.4.3 Public Access and Open Source Software

Public licenses — CC and GPL
CREATIVE COMMONS (CC)
  For ANY creative work (websites, music, film, literature).
  Lets the author give others the right to share/use/build on a
  work, while the AUTHOR still decides the exact licensing conditions.

GNU GENERAL PUBLIC LICENSE (GPL)
  Specifically for SOFTWARE.
  Grants end users the freedom to run, study, share AND MODIFY the
  software -- and these same freedoms must be PRESERVED in every
  derivative work. Distributors MAY charge a fee or give it free.
FOSS vs. Freeware vs. Proprietary (tying back to Chapter 1)
FOSS         source code OPEN -- anyone can access, modify, improve
             e.g. Ubuntu, Fedora, LibreOffice, Mozilla Firefox
FREEWARE     free to USE, but source code is CLOSED/not shared
             e.g. Skype, Adobe Acrobat Reader -- personal use OK,
             commercial redistribution is NOT
PROPRIETARY  copying prohibited by copyright law; must be purchased

Software piracy — unauthorised copying/distribution of licensed software, whether for sale, free distribution, or even just your own extra copy — is copyright infringement regardless of intent, and degrades both system performance and the wider software economy.

11.5 Cyber Crime

A cyber crime is one where the computer is either the target or the tool of the offence.

11.5.1 — Hacking: White hat vs. Black hat
WHITE HAT (ethical hacker)
  Finds security loopholes deliberately, then REPORTS them to the
  owner -- improves the system's security rather than exploiting it.

BLACK HAT (cracker, non-ethical hacker)
  Breaks through security for identity theft, monetary gain, to
  bring down a rival site, or leak sensitive data.
11.5.2 — Phishing, email spoofing & identity theft
PHISHING   fake websites/emails that LOOK authentic, used to collect
           usernames, passwords, banking/credit card details

EMAIL SPOOFING  the most common phishing method -- a forged sender
                address made to look like it's from your bank/school

IDENTITY THEFT  stolen personal data (demographic, banking, PAN,
                Aadhaar) misused by the thief:
   - Financial identity theft -- for monetary gain
   - Criminal identity theft -- to hide the criminal's real identity
   - Medical identity theft -- to obtain drugs/treatment
11.5.3 — Ransomware
Attacker gains access, ENCRYPTS the victim's data, then demands
payment to restore access -- or threatens to leak sensitive data/
photos otherwise. Spreads via malicious sites, doubtful software
downloads, spam email attachments, or malicious ads.
11.5.4 — Prevention checklist
- Regular data backups
- Keep antivirus software updated
- Never use pirated software; download only from secure (HTTPS) sites
- Keep browser and all application software updated
- Avoid untrusted websites and their downloads
- Use strong, PERIODICALLY CHANGED passwords -- different per site,
  avoiding common words/names
- Don't let browsers save passwords/auto-fill on a shared computer;
  use private/incognito browsing there
- Don't accept cookies on unknown sites when asked Yes/No
- Shop/transact only on well-known, secure sites
- Secure your home wireless network with a strong, regularly changed password

11.6 Indian Information Technology Act (IT Act)

India's IT Act, 2000 (amended 2008) gives legal guidelines for processing, storing and transmitting sensitive information, and legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures. Cyber cells in police stations handle complaints; a Cyber Appellate Tribunal resolves disputes like source-code tampering, hacking, unauthorised password use, and publishing someone's sensitive data without consent. The Act also lets government departments accept official documents purely in digital form.

Digital signatures, briefly

The digital equivalent of a paper certificate — built on a unique digital ID issued by a licensed Certified Authority (CA) under Section 24 of the IT Act. Signing digitally attaches a verifiable identity to a document.

11.7 Impact on Health

Heavy screen time can cause real physical and psychological strain. Ergonomics — the science of arranging workplaces/furniture/equipment safely and comfortably — helps reduce this.

Common health effects & mitigations
EYE STRAIN       from screen glare, worse on small handheld devices
                 -- periodically look at distant objects; take outdoor breaks

BAD POSTURE /
BACK, NECK,      corrected by ergonomic seating/desk/screen positioning
SHOULDER PAIN    (90° angles at wrist, seat-back, knee; proper viewing
                 distance ~19-24 inches, viewing angle ~30°)

WRIST/FINGER     from prolonged, poorly-aligned keyboard (physical or
PAIN             touchscreen) use -- may need medical attention if severe

STRESS, FATIGUE, broader effects of excessive digital-device time
OBESITY
Device care checklist (keeps the system itself healthy)
- Clean regularly with a solution MADE FOR electronic screens
- Wipe the monitor with a soft microfibre cloth
- Keep away from direct heat/sunlight; ensure ventilation
- Never eat/drink over the keyboard -- crumbs and spills damage it

8. Exercise (from the textbook — representative subset)

NCERT Exercise, Chapter 11

Work through these — mostly scenario-based reasoning

  1. Revaan uses Atharv's still-logged-in email to send inflammatory messages to classmates. Which cyber crime is this — hacking, identity theft, cyber bullying, or plagiarism? Justify.
  2. Rishika finds Garvit's crumpled email/password on paper. What is the ethically correct action: tell Garvit, share it with classmates, or use it herself?
  3. Shaurya downloads a 2-minute clip on Python tuples, adds "Prepared by Shaurya," and emails it to a sick classmate. Is this fair use, hacking, copyright infringement, or cyber crime?
  4. After a fight, which of these is not cyber bullying: an apology email; a threatening "don't call me" message; or uploading an embarrassing edited photo of a friend?
  5. For a "Digital India" school project, which counts as plagiarism/infringement: rephrasing a paragraph in your own words and citing it; making a photo collage from downloaded images; or pasting a downloaded official icon on your cover page? Justify each.
  6. Match: Plagiarism / Hacking / Credit card fraud / Digital Footprint — against: "copy-paste from the Internet," "breaking into computers to read private files," "fakers requesting bank details via rewards," "the trail left when using the Internet."
  7. You receive an SMS claiming to be your bank, asking you to reply with your PIN to verify a transaction. Should you SMS your PIN? Should you instead call the bank's official helpline?
  8. Preeti deletes a birthday photo from a social site after a fight with Himanshu, but later finds he still has and forwarded it. Why could he still access it? Could others too? Was it really deleted from her digital footprint?
  9. Differentiate: (a) Copyright vs. Patent, (b) Plagiarism vs. Copyright infringement, (c) Ethical vs. non-ethical hacking, (d) Active vs. passive digital footprints, (e) Free software vs. Free and Open Source Software (FOSS).
  10. What steps should you take before reusing a short web-article excerpt, to properly credit the source?
  11. Will you share your home wifi router's password with: parents, friends, neighbours, home tutors? Justify each answer.
  12. Write a short note on the health hazards of inappropriate/excessive laptop use, and how to mitigate them (for a parent explaining this to a child who just got a laptop).
